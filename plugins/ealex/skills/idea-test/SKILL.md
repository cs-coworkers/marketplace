# Idea test

An idea is a stack of assumptions. Find the ones that would kill it, write each so the world can say
no, and test the riskiest first, as cheaply as possible.

## Steps
1. **Read** `hypotheses.md` (don't duplicate premises), `goals.md`, `now.md`.
2. **If there are several ideas,** rank them first: for each, score 1–5 on pain (how costly the
   problem is), reach (can the founder get to these customers), founder fit (why them), money (will
   someone pay, how much), and speed (how fast to a real signal). Recommend one and say what you'd drop.
3. **List the assumptions** behind the chosen idea: customer, problem, solution, willingness to pay,
   channel, cost to deliver, why now. Ask the founder to add any.
4. **Rank by risk:** how likely it is to be false × how fatal if false. Take the top 3–5.
5. **Write each as a falsifiable premise:** "[Who] will [do what measurable thing] [by when / at
   what rate]", plus the **kill line** (the result that means it's false), agreed before the test.
   Example: "At least 4 of 10 operations leads at 20–100 person logistics firms will book a second
   call after a 15-minute problem interview within 2 weeks. Kill line: fewer than 2."
6. **Cheapest test per premise**, from the ladder (cheapest first): desk research → 5–10 problem
   interviews (**interview-synthesis**) → a landing page or waitlist with a real ask → a
   concierge or manual version for 1–3 customers → a pre-sale, deposit or letter of intent → a
   timeboxed MVP. Pick the lowest rung that can hit the kill line. Give cost, time and owner.
7. **Timebox** each test (default 2 weeks) and say what happens on each outcome.
8. **Propose** the `hypotheses.md` rows: premise, kill line, test, start date, timebox, starting
   confidence (with one line of reasoning), status `live`. Write after a yes. The weekly review then
   tracks them.

## Rules
- A premise without a number and a date isn't one; rewrite it.
- Opinions and compliments are not evidence; commitments (time, money, reputation) are.
- Landing-page copy and ads for a test go to Sam if the member has Sam; Alex writes the test design.
- Say "kill" when the evidence says kill.
